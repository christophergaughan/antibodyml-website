AntibodyML Consulting LLC

A research practice in antibody developability.

Protein design has outrun protein manufacturing. The work here sits in the gap — asking which computationally designed molecules will survive contact with a real process, and building the structural, thermodynamic, and machine-learning methods needed to answer that before anyone orders a gene.

Current work

Three open questions

Each of these is a live research thread rather than a finished product. Methods and negative results are published alongside positive ones.

Can a generative model stand in for molecular dynamics?

In progress · manuscript near submission

Deep-learning emulators now produce equilibrium conformational ensembles directly, without integrating any equations of motion. BioEmu is one such model. If the ensembles are physically faithful, a calculation that costs weeks of graphics processing unit (GPU) time collapses to minutes — which changes what is practical to ask during early screening.

Whether they are faithful is the harder question, and the interesting part is how you would know. A model trained on the Protein Data Bank, on predicted structures, and on hundreds of milliseconds of simulation may be reproducing physics, or it may be recalling a near neighbor. For a target with a close homolog in the training set, those two cases make identical predictions, so agreement with experiment cannot distinguish them. A benchmark that does not control for training-set overlap is measuring recall and calling it accuracy.

The current work builds that control: matched comparisons against explicit-solvent molecular dynamics (MD) using the AMBER ff19SB force field with OPC water, on systems selected so that overlap with the emulator's training data can be checked directly rather than assumed.

Do developability tools mis-score single-domain antibodies?

In progress · manuscript in preparation

Camelid single-domain antibodies — VHH domains, often called nanobodies — are a heavy-chain-only fold. The β-sheet face that in a conventional antibody is buried against the light chain is, in a VHH, exposed to solvent and substituted with hydrophilic residues. It is a surface with no counterpart in a conventional Fv.

Nearly every developability scoring method in current use was parameterized on conventional heavy–light antibodies. Pointed at a VHH, those methods encounter an exposed framework surface that nothing in their training distribution resembles. The question is whether this produces systematic mis-ranking — and if it does, whether the error localizes to that former interface or is spread across the domain.

The answer matters practically. VHH domains are increasingly produced by generative design, and the tools used to triage those designs may be penalizing exactly the features that make the fold work.

Which designed glycosylation sites are actually occupied?

Published, with follow-on tooling

An N-X-S/T sequon in a variable domain is a liability only if the cell's oligosaccharyltransferase can reach it. Sequence-level scanning flags every sequon; structure determines which ones matter. A survey of 19,265 human antibody structures (published 2026, below) measured how often variable-domain sequons are occupied and re-examined a long-standing hypothesis about which residues suppress occupancy.

The practical output is a scanner that takes designed variable-domain sequences and separates sequons likely to be occupied from those that are structurally inaccessible, with explicit uncertainty on the call rather than a binary flag.

Method

Where the free energy goes

Binding affinity is a free energy, and most of the difficulty in predicting it lives in the term that structures cannot show you.

ΔG°bind = ΔH° − TΔS° Association equilibrium at constant temperature and pressure

Affinity engineering overwhelmingly targets the first term. Add a hydrogen bond, improve shape complementarity, bury more surface. Enthalpic contributions are local, visible in a crystal structure, and tractable to reason about one contact at a time.

The entropy term is where the difficulty actually is. On association it carries three contributions pulling against each other:

  • Translational and rotational — strongly unfavorable

    Two freely tumbling bodies become one. Estimates of the cost cluster somewhere around 10–20 kcal mol⁻¹ of −TΔS at 298 K, but that spread is not measurement error: it reflects real disagreement about how much residual motion survives inside a complex. Any method that reports this term to two decimal places is reporting its own assumptions.

  • Conformational — unfavorable when flexible elements order

    Anything mobile in the free state that becomes fixed at the interface pays for it. A long, flexible complementarity-determining region that rigidifies on binding pays a great deal. This term is a property of how the whole ensemble changes, not of either endpoint structure.

  • Solvent — favorable, and the origin of the heat capacity change

    Burying apolar surface releases structured water into bulk, which is entropically favorable and scales roughly with buried apolar area. It also produces the negative change in heat capacity that makes both ΔH and ΔS temperature-dependent — so an affinity measured at 25 °C does not transfer cleanly to 37 °C.

Enthalpy–entropy compensation

In aqueous systems these terms are not independent. A modification that improves ΔH routinely costs TΔS, and ΔG moves far less than the structural change suggests it should. This is the single most common reason a rationally designed affinity improvement fails to materialize, and it is why decomposing ΔG is more useful for understanding a result than for predicting one.

Of the three terms, the conformational one carries the most design latitude and is estimated the worst. Translational and rotational losses are close to fixed for a given association. Solvent terms follow buried surface area and can be approximated from geometry. Conformational entropy requires the ensemble — and from molecular dynamics it converges slowly and expensively, which is precisely why it gets dropped from fast screening pipelines.

This is where the two threads above meet. Conformational entropy is an ensemble property, and relative populations give relative free energies directly:

ΔG = −RT ln(p1 / p2) Free energy difference from the relative populations of two states

If a generative emulator produces faithful ensembles, that estimate becomes cheap and the term stops being the one everyone skips. If the emulator is recalling rather than computing, it yields a confident number that means nothing at all — and the number will look perfectly reasonable either way. Which is the entire argument for building the benchmark before building anything on top of it.

About

Who does this work

AntibodyML Consulting LLC is the research practice of Christopher L. Gaughan, Ph.D., a chemical and biochemical engineer working across structural biology, molecular simulation, and machine learning.

The background is deliberately two-sided. The wet-lab half is real: protein purification and chromatography, affinity and size-exclusion separations, immunoassays, antibody conjugation. The computational half runs from large-scale molecular dynamics on national supercomputing allocations, through deep learning models built and trained from scratch, to production machine-learning systems deployed on cloud infrastructure.

That combination is the whole point. Developability questions go wrong when they are answered by someone who has only ever seen the molecule as a string, or only ever seen it in a column.

The governing commitment is to the result rather than the story: methods are published so they can be checked, uncertainty is stated rather than implied, and findings that fail to replicate are reported as such.
