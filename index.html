Computational antibody developability.

Protein design has outrun protein manufacturing. AntibodyML works on the gap between a sequence a model proposed and a molecule a process can actually make — using structural analysis, molecular dynamics, and machine learning to find the liabilities before they reach the bench.

What we do

Three kinds of problem

Engagements are scoped as analysis, not seat licenses. Every result ships with the notebook that produced it and the uncertainty around it.

Developability risk

Sequence- and structure-level assessment of what will cause trouble downstream.

  • N-linked glycosylation liability in variable domains
  • Sequon scanning for AI-designed sequences
  • Risk ranking with explicit uncertainty bounds

Structural & dynamics analysis

Where a static structure is not enough to answer the question being asked.

  • AMBER molecular dynamics on GPU infrastructure
  • Conformational ensembles and generative structure models
  • Single-domain antibody (VHH) framework analysis

ML infrastructure

Research code made reproducible, and pipelines that survive contact with production.

  • AWS Batch, SageMaker, S3, ECR, CloudWatch
  • Terraform, Docker, Kubernetes
  • Reproducible notebooks and containerized workflows

About

Who you would be working with

AntibodyML Consulting LLC is the practice of Christopher L. Gaughan, Ph.D., a chemical and biochemical engineer working at the intersection of structural biology, molecular simulation, and machine learning.

The background is unusually two-sided. The wet-lab half is real: protein purification and chromatography, affinity and size-exclusion work, immunoassays, antibody conjugation. The computational half runs from large-scale AMBER molecular dynamics on national supercomputing allocations through deep learning models trained from scratch, to production ML systems deployed on AWS.

That combination is the point. Developability questions fail when they are answered by someone who has only ever seen the molecule as a string, or only ever seen it in a column.
